The honest part

I deliberately won’t teach you everything.

About one concept in ten is held back. You never see it, never review it — it just sits on your map as a question mark for thirty days.

The day-30 test covers both. If the taught concepts score far higher, the teaching did that. If the two come out level, it didn’t work, and I’d rather find that out with ten people than with a thousand.

Almost no learning product collects this comparison, because it is the one number that can say the product is useless.

And then the harder test

Day 31 to 45, everything goes quiet.

No cards, no sessions, no reminders. Then one more test, unannounced, on day 45.

This is the one I actually care about. Remembering something on day 30 while you’re still practising it proves far less than you’d think. Remembering it after a fortnight of silence is the thing everyone claims and nobody checks.

What you won’t find in it

No streaks. No badges. No one asking how you like to learn.

Learning styles aren’t real
Matching teaching to “visual” or “auditory” learners has failed every controlled test it has been given. You will never be asked. What you actually remember is the only signal used.
Points you can’t farm
Your score moves only when you recall something correctly at least a day later. Showing up earns nothing, because showing up isn’t what’s being measured.
Confidence is data, not truth
You’ll rate how sure you are before each answer, then see how often “certain” was wrong. Feeling like you know something is the most reliable illusion in learning, and it is worth seeing your own.

Forty-five days, and you’ll know whether it stuck.

With a number rather than a feeling — which is more than I can say for anything else either of us has tried.
