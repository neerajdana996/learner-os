New concept · Why a geometric series converges
How to hold it
The trick is one line long and it is worth memorising as a move rather than a result: multiply the sum by r, subtract it from itself, and almost everything cancels.
S = a + ar + ar2 + ar3 + …
What we want, written out.
rS =      ar + ar2 + ar3 + …
Multiply through by r. Every term shifts one place right.
S − rS = a
Subtract. Everything after the first term appears in both rows and cancels — this is the whole idea.
S(1 − r) = a
Factor.
S = a1 − r for |r| < 1
Divide — and here is the debt. The cancellation in step three quietly assumed the tail goes to zero. It only does when |r| < 1.
What the formula says if you ignore the condition
1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + … = 11 − 2 = −1
Every algebraic step above is valid, and the answer is absurd. The condition is not a footnote attached to the result — it is the thing that made step three legal in the first place.
Remember the move, not the fraction. If you can do the multiply-and-subtract you can rebuild the formula in ten seconds; if you only memorised a/(1−r) you will use it on a series that diverges.
